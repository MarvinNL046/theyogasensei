When building pages from the `/public/images/design-templates/` references, work **one page at a time** and study the reference image in detail before writing code. Marvin called out a rushed first homepage attempt as "een beetje ff snel gemaakt, lui aan" — the structure was roughly right but I missed obvious template details (vertical dividers between trust pillars, italic second-line of hero headline, product photography vs person photography in topic grid, vertical Japanese edge text, separate Sensei Picks as product cards instead of recycled article cards).

**Why:** the design templates are the source of truth and represent careful art-direction decisions. Skipping past them with "good enough" structure undercuts the premium editorial feel that defines the brand.

**How to apply:**

- Before writing a single line of layout code, **read the template image carefully** and write out a detailed section-by-section breakdown of what's actually there (eyebrows, dividers, italics, image content, vertical accents, spacing rhythm).
- **Surface every gap explicitly** — missing imagery, copy decisions, anything ambiguous — and ask before assuming.
- Do **one template per session** unless the user explicitly asks to batch.
- After building, re-open the template image and walk through it section by section to confirm match before declaring done.
- Person photos vs object/product photos is a real distinction in the _final_ design — but while building Phase 1, **use the Aiko persona photos we already have** even where templates show products. More photography is coming later. Marvin clarified: "het gaat om het design" — focus is layout/typography/spacing/dividers/vertical accents, not asset fidelity yet.

See [[design-system-decisions]] for the underlying palette/typography decisions; this feedback is about _execution discipline_, not the tokens.
