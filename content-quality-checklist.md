# The Yoga Sensei Content Quality Checklist

Last updated: 2026-05-24

Use this checklist before publishing or briefing content for The Yoga Sensei. It is written for practical SEO execution: what must never be skipped, what to check before publish, and what matters most in the first 90 days.

## The 5 Red Lines

Never cross these.

### 1. Do Not Invent Data

Never write claims like:

- "We tested"
- "9/10 score"
- "Lasted 6 months"
- Exact prices
- Review counts
- Star ratings
- Aggregate ratings

Only use those claims when The Yoga Sensei has real evidence or a verified source. If not, use transparent language:

- "researched"
- "selected"
- "compared"
- "reviewed from public product data"
- "based on publicly available product specs"

### 2. Do Not Invent PAA Questions

If a question was not actually seen in the live SERP or captured from a named SEO/SERP source, do not call it People Also Ask.

Use:

- `Verified PAA Questions`
- `Related Questions From Source Research`
- `FAQ Candidates`

This keeps future content audits clean and prevents optimizing around invented SERP data.

### 3. Do Not Make Medical Claims

Never claim yoga cures, treats, fixes, prevents, or guarantees relief for pain, injuries, anxiety, blood pressure, digestion, sleep, or chronic conditions.

Use cautious wording:

- "may help"
- "may support"
- "some people find"
- "is often used to support"
- "some studies suggest"

For health, pain, senior, kids, or medical-adjacent content:

- Add a medical disclaimer near the start.
- Add safety guidance where relevant.
- Link to reliable sources.
- Avoid treatment promises.

### 4. Do Not Hide Affiliate Links

Affiliate content must include a clear disclosure before affiliate CTAs or product sections.

Good disclosure:

> We may earn a commission when you buy through links on our site. This never affects our recommendations.

Keep affiliate CTAs useful and calm:

- "Check Price"
- "View Review"
- "See Current Price"
- "Compare Options"

Avoid pressure copy like "Buy Now!!!"

### 5. Write For The Reader First

If there is tension between "what might rank" and "what genuinely helps the reader," choose the reader.

Every page should answer:

- What does the reader need?
- What decision or action can they take next?
- What would make this page more useful than the current top results?

## Pre-Publish Checklist

### Keyword And Intent

- [ ] Primary keyword appears naturally in the title tag.
- [ ] Primary keyword appears naturally in the H1.
- [ ] Primary keyword appears in the first 100-150 words.
- [ ] URL slug is short and readable.
- [ ] Meta description includes the keyword or close variant.
- [ ] Keyword is not stuffed. Keep usage natural.
- [ ] Secondary keywords are used naturally in H2s, H3s, and body copy.
- [ ] Search intent matches the top-ranking format.
- [ ] Cannibalisation check is complete.
- [ ] Merge candidates are handled with canonical or consolidation decisions.

### On-Page SEO Basics

- [ ] Title tag is roughly 50-60 characters when possible.
- [ ] Meta description is roughly 140-160 characters when possible.
- [ ] Meta description has a useful hook or clear benefit.
- [ ] Only one H1 exists.
- [ ] H2/H3 hierarchy is logical.
- [ ] No H3 appears without a relevant H2 parent.
- [ ] Every meaningful image has descriptive alt text.
- [ ] Decorative images use empty alt text: `alt=""`.
- [ ] Internal links point to 3-5 relevant pages where possible.
- [ ] Internal link anchors are descriptive, natural, and varied.
- [ ] External links point to reliable sources where needed.
- [ ] External source links use descriptive anchor text, not "here" or "this study."
- [ ] FAQ section uses verified PAA or clearly labelled related/source questions.

### Technical Basics

- [ ] Page loads quickly on mobile.
- [ ] Layout is mobile-friendly.
- [ ] Images are compressed and appropriately sized.
- [ ] Image filenames are descriptive and kebab-case.
- [ ] Images have stable width/height or reserved layout space.
- [ ] Hero/LCP image is not lazy-loaded.
- [ ] Below-fold images are lazy-loaded.
- [ ] Canonical tag is correct.
- [ ] No broken internal links.
- [ ] No broken outbound links.
- [ ] Structured data matches the visible page content.
- [ ] Schema does not include fake ratings, review counts, prices, tests, or medical claims.

### Content Quality

- [ ] Main question is answered in the first 100-150 words.
- [ ] Word count is competitive with the top results, but not padded.
- [ ] The page adds something the top results do not.
- [ ] The page includes concrete next steps.
- [ ] Examples, tables, images, or routines are genuinely useful.
- [ ] Content is simple enough for a beginner to understand.
- [ ] Jargon is explained.
- [ ] Author bio or author context is visible where appropriate.
- [ ] "Last updated" date is visible for content that needs freshness.

## Affiliate Content Extras

Use this for product roundups, reviews, comparisons, and buying guides.

- [ ] Affiliate disclosure appears before product CTAs.
- [ ] Product claims are source-backed.
- [ ] Exact prices are avoided unless verified and maintained.
- [ ] Pros and cons are honest.
- [ ] Each product includes "who it is for" and "who should skip it."
- [ ] Comparison table appears early for BOFU pages.
- [ ] CTA buttons are clear but not pushy.
- [ ] Affiliate links use `rel="sponsored nofollow"`.
- [ ] Affiliate links that open in a new tab also include `noopener`.
- [ ] No fake tests, fake ratings, fake review counts, or fake ownership claims.
- [ ] Product schema only includes accurate, verified product data.
- [ ] AggregateRating schema is not used unless The Yoga Sensei has legitimate rating data.

## YMYL And Health Content Extras

Use this for senior yoga, pain, mobility, sleep, anxiety, digestion, injury, kids, and medical-adjacent topics.

- [ ] Medical disclaimer appears near the start.
- [ ] Safety section is included where relevant.
- [ ] "Consult a qualified professional" guidance appears where relevant.
- [ ] Medical or physiological claims are source-backed.
- [ ] Claims use cautious wording.
- [ ] No cure, treatment, prevention, or guaranteed outcome claims.
- [ ] References section is included for higher-risk topics.
- [ ] No fake reviewer, healthcare credential, RYT credential, or expert quote.
- [ ] Medicare, insurance, or program eligibility claims are verified from official/current sources.

## First 90 Days Priorities

### 1. Build Topical Authority

Choose 1-2 clusters and build them deeply before opening too many new pillars.

Good early clusters for The Yoga Sensei:

- Chair yoga for seniors
- Yoga mat reviews and mat care
- Beginner yoga support

### 2. Take Fast Wins First

Prioritize low-KD, meaningful-volume keywords where the SERP is beatable.

Examples already in the backlog:

- how to clean a yoga mat
- chair yoga for seniors
- best yoga mat for hot yoga
- best yoga poses for back pain
- yoga poses for two people

### 3. Build An Internal Linking Spider

After the first few articles, track which pages link to each other.

Each article should:

- Link up to its pillar.
- Link sideways to related cluster pages.
- Link down to supporting content when available.
- Receive links back from related pages.

### 4. Set Up Search Console Early

Google Search Console should be connected from day one.

After 4-8 weeks, use query data to:

- Refresh titles and headings.
- Add missing sections.
- Find unexpected long-tail keywords.
- Improve internal links.

### 5. Do Not Start Everything At Once

In months 1-3, focus on:

- Publishing high-quality articles.
- Building the right clusters.
- Creating Pinterest assets for visual and senior-friendly topics.

Delay bigger pushes into backlinks, YouTube, newsletters, and social unless the content foundation is moving.

## Mental Checks Before Publishing

Ask:

- Would I read this article to the end?
- Does this add something the top 3 do not?
- Can a beginner understand it?
- What action can the reader take after reading?
- Is the page honest about what we know, what we tested, and what we only researched?
- Would I feel comfortable defending every claim in this article?

## Beginner SEO Tool Stack

Useful tools:

- Google Search Console
- Google Analytics 4 or a privacy-friendly analytics tool
- Bing Webmaster Tools
- Detailed SEO Extension
- SEO Minion
- Keywords Everywhere or another keyword data source
- TinyPNG or another image compression tool
- PageSpeed Insights

WordPress-only tools like Rank Math or Yoast are useful on WordPress sites, but The Yoga Sensei is currently a custom TanStack/MDX site, so schema and metadata should be implemented in code.

## What Not To Do As A Beginner

- Do not obsess over backlinks before the content foundation exists.
- Do not publish thin AI-generated articles without heavy human editing.
- Do not publish 50 weak articles instead of 4-8 strong ones.
- Do not chase domain authority as if it were a Google ranking factor.
- Do not change strategy every week.
- Do not forget to update content after 3-6 months.
- Do not open too many pillars before existing clusters have support.

## One Practical Next Step

Maintain a 30-article content calendar with:

- keyword
- KD
- search volume
- cluster
- page type
- planned publish date
- status
- brief path
- published URL
- last updated date

Consistency matters more than constant strategy changes. A simple plan that gets followed will beat a brilliant plan that changes every week.
