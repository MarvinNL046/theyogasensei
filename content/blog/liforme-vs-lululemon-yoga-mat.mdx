These two get cross-shopped constantly, and most comparisons treat them as the same product at the same price. They are not. One is a firm, grippy alignment tool with a lifespan its own founder put a number on. The other is a cushioned, reversible mat that is heavier and lasts far longer.

A word on what this is. I own the Lululemon Reversible Mat 5mm and have practised on it near-daily since late 2024. I have never practised on a Liforme — everything below about that mat comes from Liforme's own published pages, OutdoorGearLab's testing, and long-term owners. Both of our full reviews are linked at the end.

## The short answer

Choose the **Liforme** if your hands sweat, you practise alone, and you want etched guides telling you where your feet belong. Choose the **Lululemon** if you want cushioning, two usable surfaces, and a mat that will still be here in four years.

## Grip: the Liforme's real win

This is the one dimension with a clear answer. Our review scores the Liforme's wet grip 4.8 against the Lululemon's 4.3, and [OutdoorGearLab](https://www.outdoorgearlab.com/topics/fitness/best-yoga-mat) calls it a favourite for sweaty hands. On dry grip they tie at 4.5.

Both grip from the first session — neither needs the break-in week a dense PVC mat demands.

The mechanism is also the Liforme's weakness. Its open-cell polyurethane absorbs moisture rather than letting it pool, which is what stops you sliding — and, per Healthista, it is the salt in that sweat which gradually erodes the surface. The thing that sells the mat is the thing the practice consumes.

## Lifespan: the argument nobody makes

Liforme publishes no lifespan figure, but its founder told an interviewer the mat "was calculated to start to lose its grip after between 300 and 500 uses". At five sessions a week that is roughly eighteen months.

More striking: Liforme states in writing that it offers **no warranty or lifetime guarantee**, because the mat is built to biodegrade. That is an honest position, but it is not one most buyers expect at this price.

Against that, independent testers put the Lululemon Reversible at a realistic four to six years. Mine is at eighteen months of near-daily use with the grip unchanged — the marks on it are cosmetic.

## Which should you buy?

If sweat is your problem and you practise without a teacher, the Liforme earns its price — but budget for replacing it sooner than you would like, and know there is no warranty behind it.

If you want one mat that copes with everything and will still be there in four years, the Lululemon is the safer buy. It sells direct, so there is no link here that buys it, and we earn nothing either way.
