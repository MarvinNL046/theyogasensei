These two get cross-shopped constantly. They sit in the same price tier and both put polyurethane over natural rubber, which makes them look interchangeable on a spec sheet. They are not. One grips better the moment you sweat but has a lifespan its own founder put a number on. The other is cushioned, reversible, and expected by independent testers to last several years longer.

A word on what this is. I own the Lululemon Reversible Mat 5mm and have practised on it near-daily since late 2024. I have never practised on a Liforme — everything below about that mat comes from Liforme's own published pages, OutdoorGearLab's testing, and long-term owners quoted by Healthista. Where the evidence splits, I say so rather than pick the tidier side.

## The short answer

Choose the **Liforme** if your hands sweat, you practise alone, and you want etched guides telling you where your feet belong — accepting that it is closer to a consumable and carries no warranty. Choose the **Lululemon** if you want cushioning, two usable surfaces, and a mat testers put at four to six years.

If neither of those is you, [our mat buying guide](/guides/how-to-choose-a-yoga-mat) starts further back.

## Grip: the Liforme's clearest advantage

This is the one dimension with a reasonably clear answer. Our review scores the Liforme's wet grip 4.8 against the Lululemon's 4.3, and [OutdoorGearLab](https://www.outdoorgearlab.com/topics/fitness/best-yoga-mat) credits it with excellent wet and dry grip, calling it a favourite for sweaty hands. On dry grip our scores tie at 4.5.

Worth keeping in proportion: that same test placed the Liforme sixth of nineteen mats overall. This is a clear win over this particular rival on this particular axis, not a laurel over the whole market.

Both grip from the first session. Neither needs the break-in week a dense PVC mat demands.

The mechanism is also the Liforme's weakness. Its open-cell polyurethane absorbs moisture rather than letting it pool, which is what stops you sliding — and per Healthista it is the salt in that sweat which gradually erodes the top layer. The thing that sells the mat is the thing the practice consumes.

One counterpoint the enthusiastic reviews skip: a surface engineered to hold damp skin also resists deliberate movement. Our Liforme review scores smooth transitions 3.5 for exactly that reason. If you pivot a lot, that is a real cost.

## Lifespan: read both halves of the evidence

Liforme publishes no lifespan figure. The one number traceable to the company is its founder telling an interviewer the mat "was calculated to start to lose its grip after between 300 and 500 uses" — roughly eighteen months at five sessions a week.

Owner reports genuinely split, and our full review refuses to flatten that. Owners practising sweaty vinyasa report considerable wear inside two years; a five-year owner says the grip is going "a little bit" but the mat is still sticky. Intensity decides it, not the calendar.

More striking than any number: Liforme states in writing that it offers **no warranty or lifetime guarantee**, because the mat is built to biodegrade. That is an honest position, and an unusual one at this price. What the Lululemon offers instead is simply not published either way — we are not going to invent one for it.

Against that, independent testers put the Lululemon Reversible at four to six years. Mine is at eighteen months of near-daily use with the grip unchanged; the marks on it are cosmetic.

## Who should buy neither

Two disqualifiers apply to both, and our reviews of each spell them out:

- **Latex.** Both are built on natural rubber. Liforme tells allergy sufferers to consult a doctor first, and the same caution applies to the Lululemon's base. If latex is a problem for you, neither of these is your mat.
- **You want a mat that still looks new in a year.** Both absorb skin oils and stain permanently in the hand and foot zones. Mine does, and Liforme says so on its own care page. If that will bother you, buy something closed-cell instead.

Add a third if you carry your mat to class: neither is light, and the Liforme is the heavier of the two.

## Which should you buy?

If sweat is your problem and you practise without a teacher, the Liforme earns its price — provided you treat it as a consumable and accept there is no warranty behind it.

If you want one mat that copes with everything and will still be here in four years, the Lululemon is the safer buy.
