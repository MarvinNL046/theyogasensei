I bought a Lululemon Reversible Mat 5mm in dark green in late 2024 and have practised on it almost every day since. That is roughly eighteen months of mixed hatha and vinyasa, mostly at home, on a wooden floor.

This is not a review — we already have a [full review of this mat](/reviews/lululemon). This is the thing reviews cannot give you, because they are written too early: what a mat looks and feels like after a year and a half of real use.

## What I expected to wear out first

The grip. Every mat I had read about seemed to lose its stickiness, and the polyurethane top on this one is the part that does the gripping.

I was wrong about that, and it is the most useful thing I can tell you.

## What actually happened

**The grip held.** Downward dog on damp palms works the same as it did in month two. I have not needed to replace anything, and I have not once had the slide that made me buy a decent mat in the first place.

**The looks did not hold.** The smooth top shows every sweat mark, and a year and a half of them do not fully wash out. Dark green hides more than a pale mat would — pale-mat owners have a harder time of it — but the hand and foot zones are visibly darker than the rest.

**The rubber underside is untouched.** It has never slid on the wooden floor, and it looks the same as the day it arrived.

**The cushion softened slightly.** Not enough to change how it feels in a lunge, but the mat no longer springs back quite as crisply as it did new.

## The cheap thing I should have bought first

Almost all of the cosmetic damage is sweat reaching the surface. A grip towel over the top absorbs that before the mat does, and it goes in the wash afterwards — which the mat cannot.

I bought one late. If I were starting again I would buy it in the same week as the mat.

One honest caveat: a towel needs some moisture before it grips well. Manduka tells you to mist the nubbed side before practice, and a bone-dry towel on a bone-dry mat can feel worse than no towel at all. If your hands stay dry, skip it.

Yes — but the reason is frequency, not the mat. Practising most days spreads the cost thin enough that the maths stops mattering. If I had practised twice a month, a mat at a third of the price would have served me just as well, and our [beginner mat guide](/guides/best-yoga-mat-for-beginners) covers those.

## What I would tell someone buying today

Buy the mat that suits how you actually practise, then spend a little more on the accessory that protects it. In my case that order was backwards, and the visible cost of that mistake is on the mat in front of me.
