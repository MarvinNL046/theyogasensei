The best yoga mat for bad knees depends on whether you need more padding, a firmer surface or simply a small pad alongside a mat you already like. **Our shortlist starts with the Manduka PRO 6mm for dense cushioning, Gaiam Premium 6mm for a simpler budget option, and Liforme Original for people who prioritise grip.** None is a treatment for knee pain, and there is no thickness that suits every knee.

**Before buying:** this is equipment guidance, not medical advice or a rehabilitation programme. Do not continue a painful movement because a mat feels softer. The [NHS knee-pain guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/knee-pain/) recommends medical advice for pain that does not improve, and urgent help for symptoms such as being unable to move or bear weight on the knee, severe swelling or a hot, red knee with fever.

## How thick should a yoga mat be for bad knees?

**Thickness tells you how much material is there; it does not tell you how that material feels under load.** Two mats marked 6mm can compress differently. A softer mat may feel pleasant on the floor yet less steady under a standing foot. A firmer one may feel more predictable for balance but still need local padding when kneeling.

There is no supported universal “6mm ceiling” for knee comfort. We include two 6mm options because they offer different budgets and constructions, not because an extra millimetre makes a mat unsuitable. If you are comparing a 6mm yoga mat with a 10mm exercise mat, consider your standing practice, floor and ability to return a mat that does not suit you.

For a wider comparison of materials and thicknesses, see [how thick a yoga mat should be](/guides/how-thick-should-a-yoga-mat-be). If your current mat works everywhere except during kneeling, compare [separate yoga knee pads](/guides/best-yoga-knee-pads) before replacing the whole surface.

## 1. Manduka PRO 6mm — Dense cushioning for home practice

Manduka lists a high-density, closed-cell PVC construction, with a standard size of 71 × 26 inches and a weight of 7.5 lb. The extra width may be useful if you find narrow mats restrictive. The weight is the practical drawback: consider whether you will carry it to class regularly.

The surface does not absorb sweat. That makes wiping it down straightforward, but it does not mean it cannot become slippery. Manduka recommends a towel for sweaty practice. Its grip also changes with use, so do not buy it expecting the same surface feel as an absorbent PU mat.

**Consider it if:** you want a substantial mat that mostly stays at home. **Skip it if:** low carry weight or towel-free sweaty practice matters more. Our [Manduka PRO review](/reviews/manduka-pro) explains the care and guarantee details.

## A new mat or a separate knee pad?

If you like your existing mat, replacing it may be unnecessary. A folded blanket or dedicated pad lets you change one area instead of changing the entire standing surface. Check that any added layer stays in place, and remove or reposition it before a transition if it gets in the way. If it still hurts, stop rather than adding layers indefinitely.

| Your main concern | What to compare before spending |
| --- | --- |
| Only occasional kneeling feels uncomfortable | A separate pad or blanket alongside your existing mat |
| The whole mat feels too thin for floor work | A different thickness and construction, with a suitable return policy |
| Standing feels unstable | Firmness and the floor underneath, not just extra thickness |
| Carrying a mat is difficult | Packed size and weight, including any towel or pad |
| You have ongoing knee pain | Professional advice before treating it as a shopping problem |

## What to check before ordering

- **Exact model and size:** compare the listed length, width and weight, not just the brand name.
- **Floor and stability:** assess the mat on the surface where you will use it; a soft floor changes the feel too.
- **Materials:** check every layer if you have an allergy. A cork or PU top does not rule out rubber underneath.
- **Care:** use the maker's instructions for that model. Absorbent and closed-cell surfaces need different care.
- **Returns:** check whether the seller accepts a mat after unrolling or use before relying on a home trial.

For transparency, my own mat is a 5mm Lululemon rubber mat used for hatha and vinyasa. That experience is not a test of these three products or evidence that one thickness treats knee pain. If you already own a comfortable 5mm mat, this guide is not a reason to replace it automatically.
