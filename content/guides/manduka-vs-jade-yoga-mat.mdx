These two are the classic cross-shop. Both are the mat a teacher points at when you ask what to buy. Both cost about the same. And they are built from opposite materials, which is the whole story.

One is dense PVC that seals sweat out. The other is porous rubber that soaks sweat in. Everything below follows from that one difference.

A word on what this is. I have not practised on either mat. Every number here comes from OutdoorGearLab's tested group review, from Jade's and Manduka's own published pages, or from our editorial scoring in our [Jade review](/reviews/jade) and [Manduka PRO review](/reviews/manduka-pro). Where sources disagree, I say so instead of picking the tidier answer.

## First, which Jade?

Jade currently sells two Harmony mats side by side, and most comparisons online do not separate them.

- The **Jade Harmony**, the original. Jade says it is made in the United States, under US environmental and labour rules.
- The **Jade Harmony 2.0**, the newer one. Jade says it is made in Spain, using natural rubber, FSC certified and made with renewable energy. Jade describes it as tougher and longer lasting with better grip than the original.

Both are 3/16 in thick, about 5 lb, 24 in wide, and sold in 68 in and 74 in lengths. The tested scores below are for the **2.0**, because that is the one OutdoorGearLab put through its group test. Our own review covers the original. I flag which is which wherever it matters.

## The short answer

Buy the **Manduka PRO** if you practise in one place and want one mat for the next ten years. Buy the **Jade Harmony** if grip is your only real problem, your practice is dry, and you want a renewable material.

If you sweat hard, buy neither. More on that below.

Still deciding on material first? [Our mat buying guide](/guides/how-to-choose-a-yoga-mat) starts further back.

## Grip: the row that decides it

Jade's dry grip is the best number in the test. OutdoorGearLab scored it 9.5 and called it the grippiest natural rubber it tried. Our own review lands in the same place, scoring its dry grip 4.8. It grips from the first roll-out. There is no break-in.

The Manduka is the opposite on day one. It is genuinely slippery when new. OutdoorGearLab found it improved with daily use, and Manduka's own guidance is that the slickness is simply how the closed surface behaves — so the salt-scrub trick people recommend does not do much. Plan for weeks of using a towel.

That is the trade the two mats are actually offering. Grip now, or grip that arrives later and then stays.

## The sweat claim, and why I think it is backwards

Search for these two mats and you will read the same line everywhere: Jade is the sweat mat, because open-cell rubber absorbs moisture instead of letting it pool.

The tested numbers do not support that.

OutdoorGearLab scored the Jade's wet grip 7.0, well below its 9.5 dry. Then it ran a hot-yoga simulation with heat, humidity and sweat, and reported the surface turned **noticeably slicker**, with more slipping in poses under tension. Our own Jade review is harsher still, scoring wet grip 2.5.

The Manduka is not the answer either. The same testers describe it as more slippery when wet, with decent traction under light moisture only, and recommend a microfiber towel for sweat-heavy sessions.

So the honest reading is that neither of these is a hot-yoga mat. One gets slick because sweat sits on top of it. The other gets slick because sweat soaks into it. If your classes are heated, the [open-cell versus closed-cell guide](/guides/open-cell-vs-closed-cell-yoga-mat) explains the mechanism, and the third mat in the picks above is the one built for it.

## Durability, and the guarantee nobody reads

This is the Manduka's strongest row and it is not close.

OutdoorGearLab reported that after three years of intensive daily practice, its PRO showed virtually no signs of wear — no compression, no fraying, no curling edges. Manduka backs the PRO with a lifetime guarantee, which it defines as roughly ten years of regular use, and will replace a mat that wears out under normal conditions.

Read the exclusions before you count on it. Manduka covers the PRO, PROlite, PRO Travel, PRO Squared and both Long & Wide sizes, and allows one replacement per purchase. It does not cover printed graphics, Almost Perfect mats, mats marked irregular, mats bought from discount retailers, or mats used by a studio as in-studio equipment.

Cleaning is where most people would lose it. Manduka's terms exclude damage from washing machines, vacuums and garden hoses, and from submerging the mat or exposing it to excessive water. That matters if you were planning the soak method from our [mat cleaning guide](/guides/how-to-clean-a-yoga-mat) — on a PRO, use water or Manduka's own mat wash and nothing else.

## Who should buy neither

Three groups, and these caveats are important enough to repeat after the evidence.

- **Anyone with a latex allergy should skip the Jade.** Its own page says people with rubber or latex allergies should avoid contact with the mat. The Manduka is fine here.
- **Anyone doing heated classes should skip both.** Covered above — the tested wet numbers are mediocre on one and poor on the other.
- **Anyone who carries a mat daily should think hard about the Manduka.** At 7.5 lb it stays put beautifully and travels badly.

## So which one?

If you already know you will still be practising in five years, and you practise at home, the PRO is the safer money. You are buying one mat instead of three, and the testing behind that claim is the strongest of anything here.

If grip is the specific thing that has been ruining your practice, and you are not sweating through your classes, the Jade fixes it on day one and the Manduka does not.
