Manduka sells these two mats to the same person and they are built on opposite principles. The PRO seals moisture out. The GRP Adapt pulls it in. Everything that follows — the grip, the cleaning rules, the guarantee, how long each one lasts — comes out of that one difference.

So the question is not which mat is better. It is whether your practice makes you sweat.

A word on what this is. I have not practised on either mat. Every claim below is attributed to Manduka's own published pages or to OutdoorGearLab's measured testing, and where the two disagree I say so.

## The short answer

Buy the **PRO** if you practise in an unheated room and want one mat for the next decade. It is the only one of the two on Manduka's lifetime guarantee, and OutdoorGearLab ranked it first of nineteen mats.

Buy the **GRP Adapt** if sweat is already ruining your practice. It wins the wet-grip column outright and removes the towel — and you accept a mat that is not guaranteed, cannot be disinfected, and wears faster by the manufacturer's own admission.

If you are not sure which of those you are, [our mat buying guide](/guides/how-to-choose-a-yoga-mat) starts further back.

Same footprint, 1.7 pounds apart. Three of those rows need more than a glance.

## Grip: the GRP wins the column that describes your problem

OutdoorGearLab poured a litre of water on each of nineteen mats — roughly what a person sheds in a heated class — then practised on them to score wet grip. The [GRP Adapt 2.0 took 8.5](https://www.outdoorgearlab.com/topics/fitness/best-yoga-mat), the highest wet-grip score of any mat in that field. The PRO took 7.0.

That is the whole case for the GRP, and it is a good one. It is also narrower than the marketing suggests.

On the same test the PRO finished **first overall** at 91 out of 100; the GRP Adapt finished third at 82. And on dry grip the GRP's 9.5 is strong but not the best in the field — the Manduka eKO and the Prana Verde both scored a full 10.0. The GRP Adapt is not the grippiest mat ever made. It is the mat that holds on when it is wet.

A Manduka executive described the mechanism to SELF as an open-celled polyurethane with tiny openings in the surface that let sweat in. Manduka's own care guidance puts it the same way: the GRP series is built on an absorbent open-cell surface that allows moisture to be absorbed.

## The guarantee gap is bigger than the grip gap

Manduka's [lifetime guarantee page](https://www.manduka.com/pages/product-guarantee) names the covered mats one by one: PRO, PROlite, PRO Travel, PRO Squared, PRO Long & Wide, PROlite Long & Wide.

No GRP appears anywhere on it.

That is not an oversight, and Manduka does not hide the reasoning. On its [GRP help pages](https://eu.manduka.com/blogs/faq/help-center-grp-series-yoga-mats-manduka-eu) the company says the top layer shows wear over time because of the open-cell construction and its moisture-absorbent nature — and then does something manufacturers almost never do. It routes you to its other product: if you have a more vigorous practice, Manduka recommends the PRO series, because that mat is designed to withstand it.

Read that twice before you buy. The company that makes the grippy mat is telling vigorous practitioners to buy the other one.

Manduka also publishes no lifespan figure for the GRP at all. I have seen "one to two years" quoted on a page ranking for this comparison, with nothing under it. I am not going to repeat a number nobody has sourced.

## The cleaning rules are not a footnote

The GRP's care instructions are restrictive in a way that will genuinely annoy some people, and they come straight from the product page:

- **No soap.** Manduka: do not use any type of soap for cleaning, as it will compromise the performance and integrity of the mat.
- **No soaking or submerging**, and no harsh chemicals, because both damage the moisture-absorbing surface.
- **It must dry completely before you roll it.** Manduka says not to store the mat damp, to preserve its performance and longevity.

The PRO has none of those restrictions. Sweat sits on a sealed surface, you wipe it off, and you can disinfect it — which matters more than it sounds if you share the mat or practise somewhere warm.

There is a third option this comparison usually ignores: **the PRO plus a towel.** Manduka's own position is that the PRO is not built for sweat absorption and that a towel is the answer in heated classes. That combination costs more upfront and outlives the GRP by years.

## About that salt scrub

If you have researched the PRO before, you have been told to break it in by scrubbing it with coarse salt.

Do not bother. Manduka [retired that advice on 4 March 2026](https://www.manduka.com/blogs/mandukamag/why-your-pro-mat-feels-slippery-and-what-actually-helps). Its position now is that the slickness on a new PRO is not a layer of residue that salt can scrub away — it is the nature of the surface itself, and it resolves on its own with use. The company acknowledges the method reached its own packaging and says it is officially retiring it.

What it recommends instead is unglamorous: practise on the mat, wipe it down afterwards, use a towel for sweaty sessions.

The GRP has no break-in at all. It grips from the first roll-out. For a nervous beginner that is a real difference, and it is the strongest argument for the GRP that has nothing to do with sweat.

## Who should buy neither

- **You have a latex sensitivity.** The GRP Adapt is built on PU, natural rubber and latex, and Manduka does not recommend it for latex-sensitive users. That rules out the GRP specifically, not the PVC PRO.
- **You carry a mat every day.** Neither is light. At 5.8 lb the GRP is the better of the two, but a dedicated travel mat is a different product.
- **You want the cheapest mat that works.** Both sit at the premium end. If sweat is your only problem, a grip towel on the mat you already own solves it for far less — see [how to stop slipping](/guides/best-non-slip-yoga-mat).

## Which should you buy?

If your practice is unheated and you want to buy once, the PRO. The guarantee is real, the ranking is earned, and the break-in is less of an ordeal than the internet claims.

If you are dripping by minute twenty and tired of fighting a towel, the GRP Adapt. Go in knowing you are buying a consumable with rules.
