How a top-notch pillar, subpillar, and cluster article are actually built, section by section. Apply this template to every page and topical authority compounds.

**The shape of authority:** wide at the bottom, narrow at the top. Many tiny long-tail wins funnel link equity UP to the pillar. The pillar funnels search interest DOWN into cluster articles where the affiliate links live. The hub-and-spoke is non-negotiable — without it you have a blog, not a ranking machine.

## Pillar-specific SEO rules

- **One H1.** Never two. Layout components never emit H1.
- **Target the head term in H1, page title, first 100 words, and OG title.** Vary phrasing across them — never identical.
- **Word count of 3,500+ is not vanity** — it's the floor for outranking established yoga sites on competitive head terms.
- **Internal-link OUT to 8–15 cluster pages.** A pillar with no outbound internal links is a dead-end and ranks worse.
- **Lead magnet near the end, not the top.** Aggressive top-of-page email gates depress dwell time → hurts rankings.
- **Affiliate disclosure above the fold** (FTC requirement + trust signal).
- **Update the `lastReviewedAt` every 3–4 months** even with light edits — freshness signal.

## Subpillar-specific SEO rules

- **H1 includes the year.** "2026" gets re-baked annually. Builds CTR.
- **TL;DR pick box above the fold** — answers the user's question in 5 seconds, satisfying click intent.
- **Comparison table above the fold** — table = featured snippet candidate.
- **Each product gets its own H2 + 300–400 words of original prose.** Generic "this is a good mat" text gets de-ranked as thin content.
- **First-person testing language** — "When I rolled this mat out the first time..." — E-E-A-T gold.
- **Affiliate buttons every 2 product sections** — never just one CTA buried at the end.

## Cluster-specific SEO rules

- **Answer the query in the first 100 words.** Long-tail searchers want a fast answer; bury it and they bounce.
- **Pillar back-link in the FIRST paragraph or just after the hero.** This is the highest-impact internal link for ranking the pillar.
- **First-person experience anchored early.** "I've taught Sun Salutation to over 200 beginners — these are the cues that work" beats encyclopedic third-person voice every single time, post-2024 helpful-content updates.
- **One affiliate product callout per 600 words MAX.** Cluster pages aren't review pages — over-monetizing them tanks dwell time.
- **5+ internal links per article**: 1 to pillar (always), 2–3 contextual links to sibling poses/articles, 1 to subpillar gear page, 1 to related FAQ.
- **`HowTo` or `Article` schema, never both.** Pick the dominant intent — step-by-step content uses `HowTo`.
- **Sanskrit names in italics, English in regular** — this is a yoga-niche signal that helps Google understand topical depth.

Content wins rankings; technical SEO decides whether Google can read, trust, and serve your page fast enough to rank it. With TanStack Start + SSG + Vercel + Cloudflare Images you start with strong defaults — these are the pieces you still have to wire up yourself.

## HTML semantics — use the right tag, every time

## URLs — the contract you can't break

- **Lowercase, hyphenated, ASCII only.** `sun-salutation`, not `SunSalutation` or `sun_salutation`.
- **No trailing slash on content URLs.** `/poses/sun-salutation`, not `/poses/sun-salutation/`. Pick one rule and enforce site-wide via Vercel rewrites.
- **No URL parameters on canonical pages.** Filtering/sorting use client state, not query strings.
- **301 redirects when you rename anything.** Maintain a `redirects.ts` table; CI fails if a slug changes without a redirect entry.
- **Path depth ≤ 3.** `/gear/mats/manduka-prolite` good. `/category/yoga/gear/mats/manduka-prolite` bad.
- **Keywords in the path, naturally.** `/poses/sun-salutation` not `/posts/12847`.

# 5. Scannability — write for skimmers, satisfy readers

90% of visitors scroll, scan, and stay only if their eye catches something useful within the first 5 seconds. Scannability is not a UX nicety — it's a ranking signal proxy (dwell time, scroll depth, return-to-SERP rate).

## The skimmer's eye path

```
First 5 seconds, the reader's eye lands on:
  1. H1                           → "is this even what I searched for?"
  2. Hero image                   → "does this look credible?"
  3. Byline + last-reviewed       → "is this current and from a human?"
  4. TL;DR / first paragraph      → "will this answer me fast?"
  5. Subheadings (skim)           → "does it cover what I need?"
  6. First bullet/list visible    → "are there concrete answers here?"
  7. CTA / pin button             → exit point if interested
```

You have **5 seconds** to get them past step 4. After that, you have ~30 seconds to convince them this is worth reading. Design for that.

## The 12 scannability rules

### 1. TL;DR at the top

2–4 sentence summary that answers the search query immediately. Doesn't replace the article — it pre-pays the reader's attention so they keep scrolling.

### 2. Short paragraphs

**Maximum 3–4 lines on desktop, 4–5 on mobile.** A wall of text triggers bounce. If a paragraph is longer than 60 words, split it.

### 3. Subheadings every 200–300 words

The reader's eye should never see more than ~250 words of unbroken prose before landing on an H2 or H3. Subheadings are the rest stops on a long road.

### 4. Bold the load-bearing phrases

**Not every other word** — that's noise. Bold the single phrase per paragraph that captures the takeaway. A skimmer reading only the bolded text should still get the gist.

### 5. Bullet lists where parallelism exists

Lists work when items share structure ("5 mistakes," "3 benefits," "the steps"). They fail when used to disguise weak prose. Lists with single-word items look thin to Google's helpful-content classifier — flesh items out to ≥ 8 words each.

### 6. Numbered steps where order matters

Sun Salutation is a 12-step sequence — never bullets. Use `<ol>`, not `<ul>`, and never break the visual numbering. This also maps to `HowTo` schema.

### 7. Visual chunks: image every 400–600 words

Yoga is a visual subject. A page without imagery feels academic. Insert a photo, diagram, or pose illustration every 2–3 H2 sections.

### 8. Callout boxes for high-value insights

Pull-quote, "Pro tip," "Beginner warning," "Author's note" — each one breaks rhythm and gives the eye a place to land. Tailwind + Shadcn `<Callout>` component with an icon + 1–3 sentences.

### 9. Comparison tables for any "X vs Y"

Tables earn featured snippets and they're inherently scannable. Use for: gear comparisons, style differences, technique variations, before/after metrics.

### 10. Anchored Table of Contents at top + sticky on scroll

The TOC is the skimmer's table of contents — they read it like an index. Sticky on scroll keeps it accessible without forcing a scroll-up. Mark the active section as the user scrolls (TanStack Query + IntersectionObserver).

### 11. Estimated reading time + last-reviewed date

"18 min read" sets expectations and signals depth. "Last reviewed May 2026" signals trust. Both belong next to the byline, not in the footer.

### 12. White space is structure

Generous spacing between sections, between paragraphs, around callouts. Tailwind `space-y-6` on the article body, `mt-12` before each H2, `my-8` around images. Cramped pages signal low-quality content even when the writing is excellent.

## TL;DR — apply these rules and you outrank 90% of yoga blogs

1. **One pillar per topic cluster. Don't open a second until the first has 10+ clusters.**
2. **Every cluster links UP to its pillar in the first 150 words. Every pillar links DOWN to every cluster.**
3. **One H1. H2s match search subtopics. H3s only where genuinely nested. Never skip levels.**
4. **First-person voice + author credentials + last-reviewed date + citations = E-E-A-T compounding.**
5. **Schema.org per content type — Article for pillars, ItemList+Review for subpillars, HowTo for steps, FAQPage for FAQs.**
6. **Affiliate disclosure above the fold. `/go/[slug]` for every monetized link with `rel="sponsored nofollow"`.**
7. **Pinterest pin (1000×1500) in `og:image` for every page where Pinterest is the channel.**
8. **`lastReviewedAt` refreshed every 3–4 months for pillars, 4–6 weeks for subpillars, yearly for clusters.**
9. **Internal-link density: pillars 15–25 outbound, subpillars 8–15, clusters 5–8.**
10. **Word counts: 3,500+ pillar, 2,000+ subpillar, 1,200+ cluster. Don't pad — every paragraph earns its place.**
