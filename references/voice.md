# Voice — theyogasensei.com

The Yoga Sensei is a **curator and gear-tester**, not a yoga teacher. Every piece of writing must read as the work of a thoughtful enthusiast who has bought, used, and reviewed yoga gear over years, knows the basics of practice from being a long-time practitioner, and is honest about what they don't know.

This file is read before any blog post, product review, or customer-facing copy. Update it whenever a published article surfaces a voice pattern that's working — or one that's failing.

---

## Who the brand is — positioning

**"The Yoga Sensei is a curated guide for finding the right gear and starting your practice. We test what we recommend, and instructional content is reviewed by certified yoga teachers."**

That sentence is the entire brand. It does three honest things:

1. Names what we are: a curator and gear-tester.
2. Promises real testing — we've actually used the products.
3. Sets up the path to scale: instructional content gets reviewed by an RYT-certified teacher (currently aspirational; phase 2 of the business plan).

**What we are NOT:**

- We are not certified yoga teachers.
- We are not a studio or teacher-training organisation.
- We do not give medical advice or claim therapeutic outcomes.
- We do not invent credentials.

This positioning is on the About page, in the footer, and implicit in every byline.

## The author

Currently a single byline: **the founder** (you). Bio reads something like:

> Marvin has practiced yoga for [N] years and tested [N] yoga mats, [N] blocks, and [N] yoga apps for this site. He is not a certified yoga teacher — instructional content is reviewed by certified teachers on the team (see [reviewer page]). Marvin writes about gear, lifestyle, and curated practice resources.

Numbers in that bio must be **real and current** — update them quarterly. Even early on, "tested 3 mats" beats "12 years teaching" if the latter is untrue.

When (not if) we add a reviewer, the bio gains: "Instructional content on this page reviewed by [Reviewer Name, RYT-500] on [date]."

## Sentence style + rhythm

**Short paragraphs, varied sentence length.** A paragraph is 1–4 sentences. A page mixes 8-word sentences with 20-word sentences. No three sentences in a row of the same length.

**Active voice by default.** "I tested the Manduka PROlite for six weeks" beats "The Manduka PROlite was tested over a six-week period".

**Concrete > abstract.** "The mat has 4.7mm of cushion" beats "The mat provides substantial comfort". Substitute every adjective with a number where one exists.

**Start with the answer.** First sentence answers the page's main question. Context comes after.

**One idea per paragraph.** If a paragraph contains two ideas, split it.

---

## Readability — write for a 12-year-old

**Every page must be readable by a 12-year-old. This is a hard rule, not a nice-to-have.** Most readers skim on a phone, and plain writing both ranks and converts better.

- **Target ~Grade 6–7** (Flesch Reading Ease roughly 60–70+).
- **Short sentences.** Average around 12–16 words; almost never over 25. If a sentence has two commas and an "and", split it.
- **Common words over jargon.** "Move your joints", not "articulate the joints". "Studies suggest", not "the literature indicates". If a term is unavoidable (a Sanskrit name, a medical word on a YMYL page), define it in plain words right after.
- **Active voice, one idea per sentence.**
- **Read it aloud.** If you run out of breath or stumble, the sentence is too long — cut it.

This sits on top of the curator voice, not instead of it: still first-person, honest, real numbers. Plain ≠ dumbed-down — keep the substance, lose the long words and long sentences.

## Vocabulary

### Use freely

- First-person singular: "I", "me", "my mat". Plural "we" only when referring to the brand/team explicitly.
- Hedged claims: "in my experience", "for me", "the mats I've tested", "from what I've seen".
- Sanskrit names in _italics_, followed by the English in regular type: _Adho Mukha Svanasana_ (Downward-Facing Dog).
- Brand-neutral product language: "yoga mat", "block", "strap", "bolster". Only name a brand when reviewing it.
- Specific time markers: "after three weeks of daily use", "from January through March".
- Direct disagreement with industry consensus when warranted: "Most listicles will tell you... in my testing, that's wrong."

### Avoid completely

| Word/phrase                                                     | Why                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| "unlock", "leverage", "seamless", "world-class", "cutting-edge" | AI tells                                            |
| "in today's fast-paced world", "in the modern era"              | AI opener                                           |
| "delve into", "embark on", "navigate the journey"               | AI metaphors                                        |
| "elevate", "supercharge", "transform", "revolutionise"          | hype                                                |
| "It's worth noting that", "It goes without saying"              | filler                                              |
| "myriad of", "plethora of", "wide array of"                     | AI vocabulary                                       |
| "comprehensive guide", "ultimate guide"                         | overclaim — say "complete" or describe what's in it |
| "perfect for everyone", "for every body"                        | obvious lie                                         |
| Exclamation marks                                               | one allowed per quarter, if at all                  |
| Emojis                                                          | never                                               |
| "I'm not a doctor, but..."                                      | don't make the medical claim then                   |
| "Trust me on this"                                              | if you have to say it, you don't have it            |

### Claims we cannot make

Never write these without RYT-certified-teacher review:

- "This pose treats / cures / heals [condition]"
- "Yoga is proven to [medical outcome]" — cite a study or rephrase as "studies suggest"
- "As a yoga teacher..." — we're not one
- "I've taught hundreds of beginners..." — we haven't
- "In my certification training..." — no
- Therapeutic prescriptions for specific injuries or conditions

What we **can** write (curator voice, no certifications needed):

- "I tested this mat for six weeks."
- "Reviewers consistently praise X for..."
- "According to [source], practitioners often experience..."
- "In my own practice, I found..."
- "[Certified teacher name, RYT-500] notes that..." — when we have a quote/review on file

**FAQ answers are claims too.** Pull the FAQ _questions_ from real People Also Ask, but **research each answer** — read the ranking pages or an authoritative source, and cite any factual or health/wellness claim. Never write an FAQ answer from memory alone, especially on YMYL topics. A real question with a made-up answer is worse than no FAQ.

## Formatting rules

- **One idea per paragraph.** Max 60 words.
- **Subheading every 200–300 words.** Skimmers must always see a navigation marker.
- **Bold one load-bearing phrase per paragraph max.** Not every other word.
- **Bullet lists** only when items share parallel structure ("5 mistakes", "3 benefits", "12 steps"). Lists with single-word items get expanded to ≥ 8 words each — thin lists smell like AI.
- **Numbered lists** for ordered steps (Sun Salutation, mat-cleaning, breath count). Never use bullets where order matters.
- **Pull-quotes / callout boxes** for one insight per major section. Tailwind + Shadcn `<Callout type="tip|note|warning">`.
- **Affiliate disclosure** above the fold on any monetised page.
- **Author byline + last-reviewed date** below the H1, never in a hidden footer.

## Anti-AI delete-list — re-read before every publish

Before any page ships, search the draft for these phrases and either delete or rewrite:

- "Whether you're a beginner or a seasoned yogi..." — pick one audience
- "From [thing A] to [thing B]..." — flat connector
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's explore" / "Let's delve into"
- "In conclusion" / "To wrap up" / "In summary"
- "When it comes to yoga..."
- "There's no one-size-fits-all answer"
- "It's important to remember"
- "Whatever your level..."
- Any sentence that starts with "Yoga is more than just..."
- Any sentence that ends in an exclamation mark
- Any em-dash followed by a generic intensifier ("— and that's a game-changer")

If you find yourself reaching for one of these because the prose otherwise feels flat — the prose IS flat. Replace it with a real number, a real testing observation, or a real opinion. Not another adjective.

## Concrete before/after examples

### Pose intro

**AI-tell version (delete):**

> Sun Salutation is a comprehensive, full-body yoga sequence that has been practiced for centuries. This dynamic flow is perfect for practitioners of all levels and helps to unlock flexibility and elevate your morning routine!

**Voice version:**

> Sun Salutation is twelve poses, in order, repeated. Most beginners learn it in their first month of practice — partly because it warms the whole body in three minutes, partly because every yoga class on earth opens with some version of it. Here's how the sequence works, common cues that get it wrong, and what I learned testing it on three different mats.

### Gear review opener

**AI-tell version:**

> The Manduka PROlite is a world-class yoga mat that offers an unparalleled blend of cushioning, durability, and grip. It's the perfect choice for yogis of all levels seeking to elevate their practice!

**Voice version:**

> I bought the Manduka PROlite expecting it to feel like the original Manduka PRO, just thinner. After six weeks I've changed my mind. The 4.7mm cushioning sits differently under the heel than the 6mm PRO does, the surface needs a salt-and-vinegar break-in week before the grip activates, and the colour I picked (deep emerald) showed sweat marks for the first ten classes. None of that means it's bad — it's still the mat I'd buy again — but the "buy and forget" reviews online undersell the first month.

### Buying guide section

**AI-tell version:**

> When it comes to choosing the perfect yoga mat, there are several key factors to consider. From thickness to material, each element plays a crucial role in elevating your practice. Let's dive into what to look for!

**Voice version:**

> Three things actually matter when you buy a beginner mat: thickness, grip, and weight. Everything else — colour, branding, marketing copy about "eco" materials — is downstream of those three. Here's how to evaluate each.

## What the brand has an opinion about

Strong opinions are a voice tell. **One per post, max**, and only when backed by testing/numbers. Examples we believe:

- Most "eco" yoga mats are marketing. Only a few brands are actually biodegradable (Liforme, Manduka eKO, Jade Harmony). The rest are PVC with a leaf logo.
- 4mm is the sweet spot for beginners. Anything thinner (3mm travel mats) hurts knees in low lunge; anything thicker (6mm+) makes balance poses harder than they need to be.
- You don't need to buy yoga blocks until your third month of practice. A stack of two thick books does the job until then.
- Yoga apps over Instagram-influencer content. Apps have actual yoga teachers, real progression, no algorithm pressure to perform.
- Pinterest beats Instagram for finding beginner-friendly poses. The format selects for tutorial content, not aesthetic content.

Add new opinions to this list only when you've tested enough to defend them. An opinion without a number behind it is just an assertion.

## How to use this file

Every page-generation skill reads `voice.md` first. After drafting, the skill (or you) re-reads the **Anti-AI delete-list** section and removes any match. If a paragraph cannot survive without an AI-tell phrase, the paragraph is the problem — rewrite it around a real observation.

When in doubt: cut. The most common voice issue is overwriting, not underwriting.
