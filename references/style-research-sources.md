# Voice research sources — yoga teachers + gear reviewers to mine for style

The Yoga Sensei voice is **synthesized**, not copied. We extract sentence rhythm, cueing language, anecdote structure, and authentic anti-patterns from real teachers and curator-affiliate sites that already rank. We do **not** copy their credentials, claims, or stories.

Goal: produce `references/voice.md` that captures *how* good yoga writing sounds, separated from *what* claims a certified teacher can make that we cannot.

---

## Teachers — for sentence rhythm + cueing language (5 sources)

### 1. Adriene Mishler — "Yoga With Adriene"
- **URL:** https://yogawithadriene.com/blog/ + her video transcripts on YouTube
- **Why:** 12M+ subscribers, the gold standard for warm-conversational beginner voice
- **Extract:** how she frames "find what feels good", how she addresses anxiety/intimidation, how she gives permission to modify, her use of soft commands ("if it feels right, you can...")
- **Don't copy:** her "Yoga With Adriene" branded phrases, her dog Benji references, her specific 30-day journey structures

### 2. Kassandra Reinhardt — "Yoga With Kassandra"
- **URL:** https://www.yogawithkassandra.com/blog
- **Why:** Strong Pinterest presence (relevant to our channel), Yin yoga focus, clean structured writing
- **Extract:** how she structures step-by-step pose explanations, her no-nonsense FAQ answering style, how she handles "common questions" sections
- **Don't copy:** her Yin-specific framings unless we're explicitly writing about Yin

### 3. Brett Larkin — "Brett Larkin Yoga"
- **URL:** https://www.brettlarkin.com/blog/
- **Why:** RYT-500 teacher who teaches teachers — her writing balances technical/anatomical depth with accessibility. Closest to "expert curator" tone we want to emulate
- **Extract:** how she cites anatomical references without sounding clinical, how she structures comparison articles ("Hatha vs Vinyasa"), her use of teaching-from-experience without being preachy
- **Don't copy:** her certifications, her specific student stories

### 4. Jessamyn Stanley — author + teacher
- **URL:** https://jessamynstanley.com/ + her Substack
- **Why:** Strong personal voice, body-positive framing, direct opinions without softening
- **Extract:** how she takes a stance without hedging, how she calls out industry BS, her use of casual register inside otherwise authoritative writing
- **Don't copy:** her body-positive activism positioning (that's her brand, not ours)

### 5. Yoga Journal — editorial standards reference
- **URL:** https://www.yogajournal.com/poses/ + their articles
- **Why:** Long-running industry publication, established editorial voice for the niche
- **Extract:** their pose-page structure, the formality level they use, how they cite studies, how they balance Sanskrit + English
- **Don't copy:** their tone is often too stiff for our positioning — we want warmer

## Gear-review meta-sources — for the product-testing voice (2 sources)

### 9. Wirecutter — yoga mat reviews
- **URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/best-yoga-mat/
- **Why:** The gold standard for "I tested X products over Y weeks" voice. Their methodology language is what we should emulate exactly.
- **Extract:** their testing-methodology framing ("We tested 22 mats over 6 months"), their pick-categorisation ("Best overall / Best budget / Also great"), their negatives-included reviews (they always say what a product is bad at)

## Extraction workflow

For each source above, spend ~30 minutes reading 3–5 of their best-performing pieces (find via the source's own "popular" section or their SERP-top articles). Take notes on:

1. **First-sentence patterns** — how do they hook? Question, declarative, statistic, scene?
2. **Sentence length variation** — do they short-long-short or all-medium?
3. **Use of imperative vs invitation** — "Do this" vs "You can try..."
4. **Vocabulary register** — Sanskrit + English mix? Latin anatomy? Lay terms?
5. **How they handle uncertainty** — "may help", "in my experience", "studies suggest"
6. **Anti-patterns they avoid** — exclamations, emojis, hype words
7. **Anecdote structure** — where in the article? How long? Personal or third-party?

Compile these into a single `voice-patterns-notes.md`, then distill into `voice.md`.

## Honest scope note

These sources have **real teachers behind them** (most of them). When we extract style we are extracting *form*, not *substance*. We can copy how Brett Larkin structures a comparison article. We cannot claim her 15 years of teaching experience.

Our `voice.md` must encode this distinction: the *style* may sound similar to teacher-written content, but our *claims* are strictly what an enthusiast curator can honestly say.
