# Humour — how the brand handles wit on theyogasensei.com

The Yoga Sensei is dry, observational, and self-aware. Not jokey. Not zen-funny. Not winking-at-the-camera. Humour lands sideways — through a precise observation about the yoga industry's quirks, or through honest self-deprecation about being a curator and not a teacher. If a sentence makes you laugh out loud while writing it, it's probably too much.

**Frequency:** maybe one moment of wit per article, and only if it earns its place. A page can absolutely have zero humour and be perfect. A page with three jokes is worse than the same page with none.

---

## The register

**Yes:**

- Dry industry observation — calling out marketing speak, generic listicles, "eco" greenwashing, expensive aesthetics-over-function gear
- Self-deprecation about not being a teacher — "I'm not the person to ask whether your knee tracks correctly. I am the person to ask whether the mat your knee is on will slip."
- Honest understatement — "The Manduka PRO is fine."
- Specific, observational comparisons that imply a judgement without stating it — "The Lululemon Reversible Mat is $98. The Gaiam that performs identically is $30."
- Naming the obvious thing everyone pretends not to notice

**No:**

- Yoga puns ("namaste in bed", "downward dog days", "vinyasa-no")
- Sanskrit wordplay
- "Zen" or "om" or "chakra" jokes
- Buddha jokes — ever
- Jokes at beginners' expense
- Sarcasm aimed at readers
- Memes, GIFs, or reaction-image energy in prose
- Exclamation marks (already in voice.md, restating because it matters)
- "Lol", "haha", "you know?", "right?", or anything that signals the writer is asking for laughter

---

## What we're allowed to mock

The brand can be lightly critical of:

- **Industry marketing copy.** "Every yoga mat is now 'sustainably sourced from regenerative Brazilian rubber farms with carbon-negative shipping.' Some of them might actually be."
- **Price-creep on basic gear.** "A foam block is foam. It costs $4 to manufacture. The premium-branded version costs $30."
- **Listicles that recommend everything.** "The 'Best 47 Yoga Mats of 2026' is a tell. Nobody tested 47 mats. Nobody needs 47 picks."
- **Hype words in product descriptions.** "If a mat description includes the words 'unlock', 'elevate', or 'transformative', the people who wrote it have never used the mat."
- **The yoga influencer aesthetic.** "There's a difference between teaching yoga and standing on a mountain in pose for the camera. We're here for the first one."

The brand should never mock:

- Yoga itself or its traditions
- Practitioners (especially beginners)
- Specific teachers or creators by name
- Sanskrit, mantras, philosophy, or religious aspects
- People for the gear they own or can't afford

## Self-deprecation patterns that work

Self-deprecation about being a curator, not a teacher, is the strongest humour vein available to us. It reinforces honest positioning while doing real work for E-E-A-T (it signals we know our limits).

**Examples:**

- "I am not the right person to tell you whether your sun salutation form is correct. The teachers in the apps below are. I'm here to tell you which apps actually have good teachers."
- "A certified yoga teacher could tell you exactly how this pose should feel in the hips. I can tell you which mat made it less likely my hands would slip while I figured that out."
- "I've practiced yoga for [N] years. That makes me a long-time beginner, which is the demographic this site exists for."

The point isn't to be self-effacing for its own sake — it's to mark precisely what you're an authority on (gear, curation, comparison) and what you're not (teaching, anatomy, therapeutic claims).

## Concrete examples — voice vs over-humour

### Voice (right amount of wit)

> The Manduka PRO is fine. The Manduka PROlite is also fine. After six weeks I cannot meaningfully tell them apart in everyday use, despite the $30 price gap. Spend the $30 on a strap or a block instead.

### Over-humour (too much)

> The Manduka PRO is fine — I mean, FINE fine. Like, who even needs the PROlite when the PRO is right there?! Save your $30 for a strap or a block (or, you know, more matcha 😅).

### Voice (right amount of wit, gear-failure story)

> The first time I rolled out the Gaiam Premium, both hands slipped out from under me in downward dog before I'd been on the mat for five minutes. The reviews had warned me. I had not believed the reviews. The reviews were correct.

### Over-humour (too much)

> So picture this: there I am, attempting my first downward dog, and BOOM — face-plant. Should've listened to the reviews, right?! Lesson learned: read the reviews, friends!

## The exclamation rule, restated

Zero exclamation marks per article. The only exception is when quoting someone who actually used one ("'It's amazing!' said one reviewer."). And even then, prefer paraphrasing.

If a sentence requires an exclamation mark to land, the sentence is doing the wrong work. Rewrite the sentence.

---

## How to check if humour is working

Before publishing, read the page aloud. The humour moments should pass without you noticing them — they should feel like natural observations, not jokes. If you find yourself smiling at the screen reading a line, it's probably the right level. If you find yourself laughing out loud, it's almost certainly too much.

The reader should finish an article remembering one or two sharp observations and a feeling that the writer is honest. Not remembering that the writer was funny.

## What humour fails most often look like (delete on sight)

| Pattern                                             | Why it fails                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Any sentence ending in "right?" or "you know?"      | Asking for agreement is the opposite of confident voice |
| "Spoiler alert:"                                    | overused; the spoiler isn't a spoiler                   |
| Parenthetical asides for jokes — "(I know, I know)" | Drains energy from the sentence                         |
| "Plot twist:"                                       | same problem                                            |
| Pop-culture references older than 5 years           | Date the content immediately                            |
| Pun in a heading                                    | Heading is for SEO, not your amusement                  |
| Emoji-as-punctuation                                | Already banned in voice.md                              |
| Three exclamation marks in a row in a quote         | If a reviewer wrote that, paraphrase                    |
