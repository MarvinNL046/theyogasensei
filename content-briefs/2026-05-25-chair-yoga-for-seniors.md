## Direct Answer Target

Chair yoga for seniors is a gentle way to practice yoga while seated or using a sturdy chair for support. It can help older adults start with smaller, safer ranges of motion, but it should be treated as educational movement guidance, not medical advice. Start slowly, use a stable chair without wheels, and check with a qualified professional if you have medical conditions, recent surgery, dizziness, severe pain, or balance concerns.

## Detailed Outline

### H2: What Is Chair Yoga?

- Define seated chair yoga and chair-supported yoga
- Explain who it may suit: beginners, people who struggle getting to the floor, older adults who want a supported practice
- Clarify that it is still movement practice, not a treatment plan
- Mention that standing supported poses are optional, not required

### H2: Is Chair Yoga Safe for Seniors?

- Add visible medical disclaimer near the start
- Use a sturdy chair without wheels
- Practice on a non-slip surface
- Keep feet grounded when seated
- Stop for sharp pain, chest pain, dizziness, unusual shortness of breath, numbness, or feeling faint
- Encourage medical/professional guidance for recent surgery, osteoporosis, uncontrolled blood pressure, fall history, severe pain, or complex medical conditions

### H2: What You Need Before You Start

- Sturdy chair without wheels
- Comfortable clothing
- Water nearby
- Optional yoga mat under chair for grip
- Optional strap, towel, cushion, or folded blanket
- Avoid affiliate CTAs in this section unless product recommendations are clearly researched and disclosed

### H2: A Simple 10-Minute Chair Yoga Routine

- Minute 0-1: arrive, sit tall, breathe
- Minute 1-2: shoulders and neck, gentle only
- Minute 2-4: cat-cow and side bends
- Minute 4-6: twist and seated forward fold
- Minute 6-8: ankles, heels, toes, and seated hip mobility
- Minute 8-10: rest and breathing
- Include note: stop or reduce range whenever the movement feels wrong

### H2: Chair Yoga for Seniors Over 70

- Smaller ranges of motion
- Shorter sessions
- Longer rests
- More emphasis on stable setup and supervision if balance is uncertain
- Avoid implying age alone determines ability
